## 1. Origin and Positioning

### 1.1 The problem: a vacant arena for coupling and decoupling

In the field-theory framework, coupling relations of matter properties are described by polymorphic coupling (PC) — but PC answers "what couples", not "**where coupling happens**". This work fills that vacancy: combined with the axis-less electron hypothesis (electron = gap dweller), matter couples and decouples in the **gap layer** (the inter-layer gradient residence domain).

### 1.2 Non-circular localization

The mainstream phrase "chemistry is the behavior of valence electrons" is implicitly circular: why is chemistry about electrons — the answer "because electrons participate in bonding" explains a premise by its consequence. The non-circular chain within this framework:

- The electron is a gap dweller (axis-less electron hypothesis); the gap layer is the inter-layer gradient residence domain.
- As two nuclei (rigid-layer convergent clusters) approach, boundary fields overlap and the **gap field reconfigures**; shared gap residence = the chemical bond.
- A chemical reaction changes the gap-residence configuration while nuclei merely carry the residents along — **the physical carrier of coupling and decoupling is the gap layer**.

This does not conflict with molecular-orbital theory: MO wavefunctions are the wavefunction representation of gap residence. This is an ontological localization, not a replacement.

- **Inverted-region status corrected**: the classic claim that the Marcus inverted region is "unobserved in biology" is outdated — Marcus himself noted in his Nobel lecture that the small reorganization energy (~0.25 eV) of the photosynthetic reaction center and the inverted-region effect are functional elements [4]; direct evidence exists in PSII (−ΔG° ≈ 1.2 eV) [5] and in engineered non-derivatized azurins [6]. The source of the covalent-pathway β ≈ 0.7 Å⁻¹ value is pinned down (v1.1 addition): Davidson's review states verbatim "β value for ET through a vacuum (2.8 Å⁻¹) is much larger than for ET through a covalent bond (0.7 Å⁻¹)" [11], resting on the Regan–Risser–Beratan–Onuchic pathway-model family [12] — attributed to the Beratan/Onuchic line rather than the earlier conjectured Winkler–Gray line. ### 5.5 A tier-level explanation of the cross-species discrepancy of the RC rate prediction, and a threshold interval

In the axis-less electron line's 3D residence prediction for the reaction center, the L/M symmetry breaking of 7.6–8.5× (structural plane) is universal across species, but the quantitative rate prediction errs by ~1700× in R. sphaeroides (B. viridis fits). Cross-reading: the failing station QA→QB is exactly the least-driven station of the chain ("driven by a modest free energy", 14 Å, 10⁴–10⁵ s⁻¹ [8]; the QA⁻→QB driving force is protein-regulated and distinguishes secondary-site specificity for most substituted quinones [9]). **B. viridis (150 mV) sits in the under-driven normal-region tier (rate still modulated by driving force); R. sphaeroides (35 mV) sits fully in the gating-limited tier (rate decoupled from driving force)** — the discrepancy is not a failed prediction but the same station occupying different tiers of the sweetness spectrum in the two species. The structural plane (L/M breaking = where gap residence exists) and the functional plane (tiers = what limits the rate) are orthogonal.

**First interval estimate of the gap-formation threshold (v1.1 addition; T_gate ∈ (35, 65) mV, intracavity RC caliper)**: a three-station comparison — cyt c2→P (65–420 mV mutation span) changes rate monotonically with driving force (over 2 orders of magnitude) = gate not engaged; QA→QB at 150 mV (B. viridis) shows visible driving-force modulation = not engaged; 35 mV (R. sphaeroides) is fully decoupled = engaged. Gating engagement is observed only at 35 mV, and the lowest observation of non-engagement is 65 mV; hence the threshold interval estimate (35, 65) mV. The competing explanation (the Marcus flat region: at 35 mV the activation-energy difference is only ~0.001 eV; the near-apex region is inherently insensitive to driving force) is not fully excluded — but the strong modulation still measured at 65 mV (the Marcus term is only ~2 meV, insufficient to explain a >2-order-of-magnitude change), contrasted with full decoupling at 35 mV, supports the threshold hypothesis; discriminative power is rated moderate. **Closure design**: a quinone-substitution series at the R. sphaeroides QB site (a quinone library with midpoint potentials tunable over 67–180 mV [9]) — if a "decoupling → restored modulation" turning point appears in the 35–65 mV interval, threshold localization closes (to be executed; see §5.9).

## 6. Honest boundaries

1. **Interpretive re-reading**: this line is a field-theoretic restatement of existing chemistry (bonding theory, MO theory, transition-state theory, Marcus theory), not new physical predictions; all discriminants are re-readings of existing data.
2. **"Gap layer" in chemistry**: the in-framework name for the spatial domain of bonding electrons; no conflict with MO wavefunctions (ontological localization, not replacement).
3. **Four-state mapping**: metallic-11 and vdW-01 mappings are verified (§5.3); the ionic relay holds structurally; the "two separated clusters" reading rests on the exact five-dimer spectrum; the Xe₂ equilibrium distance lacks a first-hand value (refused to be filled from memory; left blank on record).
4. **Discriminant (i) criteria**: the pre-registered "jump = shell boundary" was too coarse; the two jump classes were distinguished empirically — the correction trail is archived; "interference fringes" remain a picture-level candidate.
5. **Applicability of the inertness discrimination**: holds within the noble family (IE1 monotonic); after the cross-family upgrade to the dual-channel function the Cl counterexample dissolves, but the EA data are periodic-table-API aggregated calipers (95 elements first-hand fetched), not traced back to original measurements element by element.
6. **Coverage of the biological-chain reading**: 20/25 stations judgeable (5 lack ΔG°, no claims made); one respiratory-chain station (a3→CuB/O₂) lacks ΔG° because multistate chemistry has no single-step value; the bL potential caliper tension (measured −100/−83 mV vs early estimate +90 mV) is on record, and this chain adopts the measured spectroscopic-titration caliper; the "functionalized division" is a post-hoc re-reading (the pre-registration only judged the normal-region share — 90% passes the line; the division table is a new structural candidate).
7. **Threshold interval estimate**: T_gate ∈ (35, 65) mV is an observational interval, not a mechanistic localization; the competing explanation (Marcus flat region) is not fully excluded; discriminative power is moderate; the closure design is on record but unexecuted; "evolutionary constraint" is an adaptivity statement, not a mechanism proof.
8. **β boundary**: a statistical magnitude, not a per-system criterion; the covalent-pathway β ≈ 0.7 Å⁻¹ is traced to the review sentence and the pathway-model family [11,12], but the three-caliper spread (0.7/0.9/1.1) is reported as is.
9. **Nitrogenase chain**: strong λ-caliper dependence (0.25–2.4 eV spans an order of magnitude); the tier verdict takes "not the inverted region" as the robust conclusion and does not lock boundary tiers; the N4 station (FeMo cofactor → N₂) potential is a textbook caliper (≈ −280 mV) not verbatim traced to primary literature, and the whole station is marked provisional; relaxation readings are based on ground-configuration differencing (excited-state populations not counted).
10. **Mg–O three projections**: Phillips 0.84 cross-confirmed by two independent literature sources [23,24]; the Bader density projection is summary-level single-point evidence (bulk ±1.65e [25]), original text not verbatim checked.
11. **D5 discriminant design unexecuted**: the three carriers are graded by actual data availability after feasibility pre-checks; carrier 1 (solid-state phase-transition spectra) is downgraded pending data-source additions.
