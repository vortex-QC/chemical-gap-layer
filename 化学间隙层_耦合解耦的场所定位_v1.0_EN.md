## Abstract

The physical arena of chemistry is implicit in mainstream chemistry: "chemistry is the behavior of valence electrons" — but asking why chemistry is about electrons, the mainstream answer "because electrons participate in bonding" is circular. Within the field-theory framework, this paper gives a non-circular localization: the electron is a gap dweller (the axis-less electron hypothesis); when two nuclei approach, their boundary fields overlap and the gap field is reconfigured; **shared gap residence = the chemical bond — matter couples and decouples in the gap layer**. Core structure: (1) the four bond types are four forms of gap residence (covalent = shared residence; ionic = unidirectional transfer relay; metallic = macroscopic network connectivity; vdW = dispersed end with no shared residence), with the Pauling ionicity continuum confirming the four forms as four anchor points of a continuous spectrum; (2) a chemical reaction is a gap-residence reconfiguration event, with the transition state as the transient intermediate reconfiguration; (3) shell structure is the discretization of the gap-residence depth spectrum, and ionization energy is the layer-by-layer depth reading. Four quantitative discriminants were executed the same day and all held: (i) NIST successive ionization energies of 38 elements, 270 levels — main-group jump points hit shell boundaries 8/8, with an extra-jump substructure in B–F revealing lone-pair repacking, and smooth stripping in Ne/Ar consistent with inertness; (ii) inertness = valence-shell saturation closure × closure strength (IE1), with reactivity onset at Xe (12.13 eV); (iii) bond-energy/adhesion-root comparison passes all three tests (halogen gradients monotonic; N-series superlinearity 5.91×; F–F lone-pair-crowding anomaly); (iv) Sabatier volcano triangle closure — gas-phase deep residence of N≡N at 945 kJ/mol is reworked at catalyst surfaces to 95–123 kJ/mol (shallower by ~8–10×) into the activation band: too weak on the Co side to activate, too strong on the Mo side to desorb; the optimum is the midpoint band of residence depth. Extended connections: a station-by-station reading of biological electron-transfer chains (25 stations, first-hand data) yields a "functionalized division of the sweetness spectrum" (normal-region transport + onset at the apex + inverted-region braking + gating-limited rate; 93% of stations in the normal region), which also uniformly explains the cross-species discrepancy of the reaction-center rate prediction. Successive 3d/4s stripping assignments (80 levels) confirm the "4s strips first" master rule and reveal dynamic rebalancing of the residence-depth spectrum (the V⁺/Co⁺/Ni⁺ relaxation channel). Chemistry (single-site reconfiguration) × catalysis (spatial sweet spot) × biological chains (temporal sweet-spot spectrum) — three tiers of discriminants converge onto one language: **the gap-residence depth spectrum**. All discriminants are interpretive re-readings of existing data; machine verification and first-hand source checking are archived throughout, with honest boundaries marked section by section.

**Keywords**: chemical bond; gap layer; residence-depth spectrum; axis-less electron; Sabatier principle; Marcus theory; electron transfer; field theory

## 1. Origin and Positioning

### 1.1 The problem: a vacant arena for coupling and decoupling

In the field-theory framework, coupling relations of matter properties are described by polymorphic coupling (PC) — but PC answers "what couples", not "**where coupling happens**". This work fills that vacancy: combined with the axis-less electron hypothesis (electron = gap dweller), matter couples and decouples in the **gap layer** (the inter-layer gradient residence domain).

### 1.2 Non-circular localization

The mainstream phrase "chemistry is the behavior of valence electrons" is implicitly circular: why is chemistry about electrons — the answer "because electrons participate in bonding" explains a premise by its consequence. The non-circular chain within this framework:

- The electron is a gap dweller (axis-less electron hypothesis); the gap layer is the inter-layer gradient residence domain.
- As two nuclei (rigid-layer convergent clusters) approach, boundary fields overlap and the **gap field reconfigures**; shared gap residence = the chemical bond.
- A chemical reaction changes the gap-residence configuration while nuclei merely carry the residents along — **the physical carrier of coupling and decoupling is the gap layer**.

This does not conflict with molecular-orbital theory: MO wavefunctions are the wavefunction representation of gap residence. This is an ontological localization, not a replacement.

## 2. Core Structure

### 2.1 Four bond types = four forms of gap residence

| Bond type | Gap-residence form | Four-state reading |
|---|---|---|
| Covalent | Shared gap residence (symmetric two-center) | 10 (local convergent residence) |
| Ionic | Unidirectional transfer (full transfer A-gap → B-gap) | Decouple + re-couple relay (10→10) |
| Metallic | Macroscopic gap network (M_conn connectivity) | 11 (network-saturated connectivity — same state as conduction) |
| vdW | No shared residence; weak dispersed-boundary coupling | 01 (dispersed) |

Metallic bonding as gap-network connectivity is directly isomorphic with the connectivity quantification line (the first discriminant object of the gap-connectivity M_conn is precisely the metallic subnet). Mixed bond types are interpolations among the four forms.

**Ionicity continuum check (§5.3)**: Pauling ionicity f = 1 − exp(−Δχ²/4) climbs continuously from C–H 0.030 to Cs–F 0.921 — the ionic/covalent dichotomy is a truncation of the two ends of a continuum; the four forms are its four anchor points and mixed bonds are interpolations.

### 2.2 Shells = the gap-residence depth spectrum

- Inner-shell electrons do not participate in chemistry = deep residence (strong near-nucleus binding); valence electrons participate = shallow residence.
- Shell structure K/L/M/N is the discretization of the gap-residence depth spectrum — isomorphic with the four-layer memory spectrum in biology and the three-cluster storage-medium spectrum: adsorption systems must stratify (the multi-layer cascade theorem), and atomic shells are its atomic-domain instance.
- **Ionization energy = the layer-by-layer depth reading** (first IE = shallowest residence depth; successive ionization = peeling layer by layer) — precisely measurable across the NIST database; the first quantitative caliper of this line.
- Electronegativity = gap-seizing capability (Pauling); redox potential = residence-transfer potential — unified in gap-layer language.

### 2.3 Chemical reaction = gap-reconfiguration event

Reaction = old-bond decoupling (residence release) → transition state (transient intermediate reconfiguration of gap residence) → new-bond coupling (residence rebuild).

The activation energy Ea of transition-state theory (Eyring) = the reconfiguration barrier; the difficulty of measuring transition-state structures (glimpsed only by femtochemistry) is consistent with the transient nature of gap reconfiguration. The rate k ~ exp(−Ea/RT) is the rate of crossing the reconfiguration barrier — residence = inverse of decay (the same language as the mass dual-roots line).

## 4. Four quantitative discriminants (executed same-day; first-hand data + machine verification)

> All data were first-hand verified (NIST ASD, PubChem, Crossref item by item), zero fabrication; discriminant scripts and raw archives accompany the reproduction package.

### 4.1 Ionization-energy spectrum = residence spectrum (38 elements, 270 levels)

- **Main structure 8/8 holds**: main-group/alkali-metal jump points all hit shell boundaries (Li@2, Na@2, Mg@3, Al@4, P@6, Cl@8, K@2, Ca@3) — direct experimental confirmation of shells as gap-residence depth stratification.
- **Sub-structure (new observation)**: B/C/N/O/F show extra @2 jumps (IE2/IE1 ≈ 3×) = lone-pair repacking; Ne/Ar have zero jumps (IE2/IE1 only 1.46–1.55×, smooth stripping) = smooth deep residence of full shells, consistent with inertness. The distinction between two jump classes (shell-boundary primary + lone-pair-repacking secondary) is an empirical finding, not a pre-registered criterion.
- The transition-metal region is irregular — see §5.7.

### 4.2 Inertness–saturation mapping (two-variable caliper)

- **Inertness = valence-shell saturation closure (configuration variable) × closure strength (IE1 depth variable)**: He 24.587 → Rn 10.745 eV strictly decreasing; reactivity monotonically non-decreasing (He/Ne/Ar = 0, Kr = 1, Xe/Rn = 2).
- Reactivity onset = Xe (IE1 = 12.13 eV, lowest of the noble family) — threshold candidate.
- Counterexample check: Cl (IE1 = 12.97 eV) is highly reactive — low IE1 is necessary but not sufficient; within-family discrimination holds, cross-family requires the configuration variable.

### 4.3 Bond-energy / adhesion-root comparison (21 bonds)

- **Test A (×2)**: H–X and C–X halogen gradients both strictly monotonically decreasing (569→295 / 481→240 kJ/mol) — electronegativity gradient = residence-depth gradient.
- **Test B**: N≡N/N–N = 5.91× ＞ O=O/O–O = 3.41× ＞ C≡C/C–C ≈ 2.65× — the N series is the most superlinear: three singly-occupied 2p residence sites minimize mutual exclusion, and the shared-residence gain is fully cashed.
- **Test C**: F–F = 154 ＜ Cl–Cl = 239 kJ/mol (within-family anomaly) — lone-pair residence-site crowding; the same two faces as Test B (N has no lone pairs = superlinear; F is lone-pair-saturated = crowding shallows).
- Data gaps honestly marked: C–C/C=C/C≡C are range/approximate values.

### 4.4 Sabatier volcano = the residence-depth sweet spot (triangle closure)

- Two independent calipers of the volcano peak at the same scale: experimental metal–nitrogen bond-energy descriptor peak 95.0±3 kJ/mol [1] × DFT N-adsorption optimum |−1.27 eV/N| = 122.5 kJ/mol (Ru(0001) short-bridge site, marked "optimum" in source) [2].
- **Triangle closure**: gas-phase deep residence of N≡N, 945 kJ/mol (confinement-class) → reworked at catalyst surfaces to ~95–123 kJ/mol (shallower by ~8–10×) into the activation band; weak side Co (54 kJ/mol) does not activate, strong side Mo (242 kJ/mol) does not desorb — **the Sabatier optimum is the midpoint band of gap-residence depth**, and the catalyst is the artificial implementation of residence-depth inter-layer transfer.
- The peak 95–123 sits near the weak/strong midpoint 148 — a "midpoint = balance" family candidate (recorded, not overclaimed).

## 5. Extended discriminants (three tiers)

### 5.1 Transition-metal mixed layers: near-degenerate residence spectra

Main-group jump points are clean (separated residence layers, sharp boundaries); transition metals (Sc–Kr) are irregular (3d/4s near-degenerate in energy, overlapping depths, crossed stripping order) — jump points are interference fringes of the depth spectrum, not boundaries. Two sub-structures:

1. Cr/Cu (3d half/full) push 4s shallow (anomalously low IE1) — inter-shell residence-site competition, the same family of mechanism as F–F lone-pair crowding.
2. Zn (3d¹⁰4s²) has the cleanest jump — the 3d¹⁰ deep-residence barrier; the ubiquity of Zn²⁺ in biology (carbonic anhydrase / zinc fingers) is the inertness expression of a full-shell deep-residence barrier.

### 5.2 The biological bridge: layered positioning of Marcus theory

- **Gating-layer isomorphism**: Hoffman & Ratner [3] (the defining paper of "gated electron transfer") confirm that the academy accepts that Marcus rates describe the transition after the gate (conformational readiness) opens. In this framework: the gate = gap formation (electron genesis), and Marcus = the propagation behavior after gap formation — layered and compatible; Marcus theory is the propagation-layer description of gap-layer dynamics.
- **Inverted-region status corrected**: the classic claim that the Marcus inverted region is "unobserved in biology" is outdated — Marcus himself noted in his Nobel lecture that the small reorganization energy (~0.25 eV) of the photosynthetic reaction center and the inverted-region effect are functional elements [4]; direct evidence exists in PSII (−ΔG° ≈ 1.2 eV) [5] and in engineered non-derivatized azurins [6]. The correct statement: **physiological ET chains are evolutionarily constrained to the normal region (center distances ≤14 Å); the inverted region is rarely visited, not unreachable in principle**.
- **β scale gap = a computable boundary between residence and propagation**: tunneling superexchange β = 1.0–1.4 Å⁻¹ [7] vs chemical hopping β = 0.1–0.3 Å⁻¹ — the 4–5× gap is the observable boundary between the two gap forms "real residence" vs "pure propagation".

### 5.3 Ionic relay and vdW zero-end verification

- **The ionic bond as a two-stage "transfer + deepening" event (Born–Haber chain)**: transferring e from the Na gap to the Cl gap costs 496 − 349 (electron affinity) = net +147 kJ/mol in the gas phase (endothermic; the transfer alone is not spontaneous); lattice electrostatic residence (Kapustinskii 746 vs Born–Haber back-solved 787 kJ/mol, 5.2% apart) supplies the remainder — **"deeper residence after transfer" is the relay driving force**; the relay mapping holds at the structural level.
- **vdW = the zero end of the residence spectrum**: He₂ ~10⁻³ / Ar₂ ~1.6 kJ/mol (accepted magnitudes) vs chemical bonds 150–950 — a 2–6 order-of-magnitude gap; vdW = no shared residence (transient correlation of dispersed fluctuations).

### 5.4 Station-by-station reading of biological ET chains: functionalized division of the sweetness spectrum

Reading 25 first-hand stations (7 in the photosynthetic reaction center + 18 in respiratory complexes I/III/IV) under a dual-λ caliper (14 stations judgeable): **normal region 12 + activation apex 1 = 93%; inverted region 1; uphill 0**.

The reading corrects the expectation that "every station sits at the activation apex" — measured rate-determining main-chain stations overwhelmingly sit in the under-driven normal region r = −ΔG°/λ ≈ 0–0.3 (complex I's potential profile is nearly flat over 7 hops); the onset station sits at r ≈ 1, and waste/protection stations at r ≫ 1. **Corrected form: normal-region transport + apex onset + inverted-region braking + gating-limited rate** — a functionalized division of the sweetness spectrum:

| Station type | r = −ΔG°/λ | Functional reading | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | ≈1 (apex) | Race the clock (ps-scale excited-state lifetime) | P*→BChl 3–5 ps |
| Transport | 0–0.3 (under-driven) | Conserve energy (ΔG° ≈ 0; energy saved for ATP) | Complex I, 7 flat hops |
| Recombination channel | ≫1 (inverted) | Brake (inverted-region slowdown prevents charge recombination) | P⁺H⁻ r = 4.4 |
| Rate-limiting | Gated | Conformational gating limits rate (ISP potential lowered 62–159 mV, rate unchanged) | bc1 k₃ = 1650 s⁻¹ |

The academy's answers on "wasteful" steps converge with this reading: complex I's flat profile = energy conservation by design; N1a is an off-pathway, not waste; N2 is an electron trap preventing ROS; electron bifurcation uses occupancy blockade; CcO short-circuits are suppressed by distance + barriers. In one language: **the residence-depth spectrum of the whole chain is arranged as a functionalized profile**. A single-site catalyst is a spatial sweet spot; a biological chain is a temporal sweet-spot spectrum.

### 5.5 A tier-level explanation of the cross-species discrepancy of the RC rate prediction

In the axis-less electron line's 3D residence prediction for the reaction center, the L/M symmetry breaking of 7.6–8.5× (structural plane) is universal across species, but the quantitative rate prediction errs by ~1700× in R. sphaeroides (B. viridis fits). Cross-reading: the failing station QA→QB is exactly the least-driven station of the chain ("driven by a modest free energy", 14 Å, 10⁴–10⁵ s⁻¹ [8]; the QA⁻→QB driving force is protein-regulated and distinguishes secondary-site specificity for most substituted quinones [9]). **B. viridis (150 mV) sits in the under-driven normal-region tier (rate still modulated by driving force); R. sphaeroides (35 mV) sits fully in the gating-limited tier (rate decoupled from driving force)** — the discrepancy is not a failed prediction but the same station occupying different tiers of the sweetness spectrum in the two species. The structural plane (L/M breaking = where gap residence exists) and the functional plane (tiers = what limits the rate) are orthogonal.

### 5.7 3d/4s successive stripping assignments: dynamic rebalancing of the depth spectrum

Stripping sequences IE1→IE5 for ten elements (80 levels, uniquely determined by ionized-configuration differencing): **4s strips first for all ten elements with zero counterexamples at the action level** — "4s = shallow residence site" directly confirms residence depth = stripping order as the master rule.

**Configuration-level finding**: the ionized ground configurations of V⁺/Co⁺/Ni⁺ are 3d^(n+1) — upon stripping 4s, the remaining 4s electron **relaxes down into 3d**: after the shallow site empties, resident electrons redistribute into deep sites. **Residence sites are not fixed orbitals but dynamic rebalancing of the depth spectrum** — precisely the microscopic carrier of the "interference fringes" of §5.1 (the 4s↔3d relaxation channel); main-group elements lack this channel (ns/np depth gap large, no crossing), hence clean jumps.

## 6. Honest boundaries

1. **Interpretive re-reading**: this line is a field-theoretic restatement of existing chemistry (bonding theory, MO theory, transition-state theory, Marcus theory), not new physical predictions; all discriminants are re-readings of existing data.
2. **"Gap layer" in chemistry**: the in-framework name for the spatial domain of bonding electrons; no conflict with MO wavefunctions (ontological localization, not replacement).
3. **Four-state mapping**: metallic-11 and vdW-01 mappings are verified (§5.3); the ionic relay holds structurally; vdW dimer exact values and the Mg–O polarity controversy remain open.
4. **Discriminant (i) criteria**: the pre-registered "jump = shell boundary" was too coarse; the two jump classes were distinguished empirically — the correction trail is archived.
5. **Applicability of the inertness discrimination**: holds within the noble family (IE1 monotonic); cross-family requires the configuration variable (Cl counterexample archived).
6. **Coverage of the biological-chain reading**: 14/25 stations judgeable (11 lack ΔG°, no claims made); bc1 station-level midpoint potentials and the NADH potential were not first-hand verified (isolated and marked); the "functionalized division" is a post-hoc re-reading (the 93% normal-region share passes the pre-registered line; the division table is a new structural candidate).
7. **Threshold not located**: whether 35 mV lies below a "gap-formation threshold" has no independent measurement; "evolutionary constraint" is an adaptivity statement, not a mechanism proof.
8. **β boundary**: a statistical magnitude, not a per-system criterion; the covalent-pathway β ≈ 0.7 Å⁻¹ was not directly verified.
9. **vdW dimers**: energy magnitudes are accepted values (He₂ ~10⁻³ kJ/mol; Ar₂ ~1.6 kJ/mol); single-point exact values were not directly verified.
